How it works

One destination. More information about the ways to get there.

01

DFLCKT maps what is known.

Known ALPR and license-plate readers, traffic-management cameras, automated enforcement cameras, and other documented roadside observation infrastructure can all matter differently.

02

Routes are compared.

Fastest is the default. DFLCKT can also show a practical lower-exposure option and the lowest-known-exposure route—even when that route is less convenient.

03

You make the decision.

DFLCKT does not decide how much your privacy is worth in minutes or miles. It gives you the tradeoff and lets you choose.

There will never be a decision made for you that won't allow you to make choices about your own privacy.

Privacy should include us

Your route is yours.

DFLCKT is being designed so it does not build a history of where you drive.

A navigation product about surveillance should not quietly become another surveillance product. Route and location information should be retained only when technically necessary for the immediate service, and precise movement history should not become a behavioral profile.

We will describe what we collect in plain language as the product develops. We will not sell precise commute or route history.

More than one kind of camera

Observation infrastructure is not all equivalent.

ALPR / plate readersDesigned to identify and index vehicle plates.
Traffic-management camerasUsed for road and incident management; recording and retention vary by system.
Automated enforcementRed-light, speed, and other enforcement systems where applicable.
Other / unknown capabilityDocumented roadside observation where capabilities are incomplete or uncertain.

DFLCKT will distinguish what is known from what is inferred. We will not say a camera “doesn't record” unless authoritative information supports that statement.
